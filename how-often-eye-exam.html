    Eye care guide

    How often should you get an eye exam?

    It depends on your age and your risk. The American Optometric Association (AOA) recommends an exam at least every two years from 18 to 64, every year from 65, and at least yearly for anyone at higher risk. The American Academy of Ophthalmology (AAO) schedule for adults without risk factors is every 5 to 10 years before 40, then more often with age, up to every 1 to 2 years from 65. Your eye doctor can tell you which fits you.

    Adults: the AOA schedule

    18 to 39: at least every two years.

    40 to 64: at least every two years.

    65 and older: every year.

    At risk, at any age: at least once a year, or as your eye doctor recommends.

    The AOA counts as at risk, among others, people with a personal or family history of eye disease, health conditions that can affect the eyes, medicines with side effects on the eyes, sight in only one eye, contact lenses, eye surgery or a past eye injury, a high or changing prescription, or work that is visually demanding or risky for the eyes. It also lists belonging to certain racial and ethnic groups.

    Adults: the AAO schedule

    For adults with no symptoms and no risk factors for eye disease, the AAO’s 2025 guideline recommends a comprehensive eye exam:

    Under 40: every 5 to 10 years.

    40 to 54: every 2 to 4 years.

    55 to 64: every 1 to 3 years.

    65 and older: every 1 to 2 years.

    The AAO also recommends a baseline eye disease screening by age 40, because early signs of disease and changes in vision often begin at that age.

    Children

    The AOA recommends an eye exam at 6 to 12 months, at least once between ages 3 and 5, and before first grade and every year after that, more often for children at risk. The AAO and the American Association for Pediatric Ophthalmology and Strabismus publish their own childhood screening guidelines. A school vision screening helps, but the AOA notes that it can’t replace a comprehensive exam.

    When to go more often

    Diabetes. The AAO recommends a first eye exam at diagnosis for type 2 diabetes and five years after onset for type 1, then at least once a year.

    Contact lenses. The AAO says to see an eye specialist every year.

    After LASIK, PRK or SMILE. The AOA recommends an eye exam every 1–2 years (see glasses after LASIK).

    A family history of eye disease. Ask your eye doctor how often to be examined.

    Don’t wait for the date

    Go sooner if your vision changes: blurry vision, squinting, eye strain, headaches or trouble seeing at night are common signs you may need glasses or a new prescription. A sudden increase in floaters, flashes of light or a shadow over part of your vision is an emergency: the National Eye Institute says to go to your eye doctor or the emergency room right away.

    After the exam

    If the exam includes a refraction, the Federal Trade Commission’s Eyeglass Rule says your eye doctor must give you a copy of your glasses prescription right away, at no extra charge, and you can buy your glasses wherever you like. It is usually valid for a year or two, depending on your state: see how long a prescription is valid. Then read your prescription and choose a frame; we make the lenses with your numbers. We don’t do eye exams ourselves.

    Questions

    Good to know

    Is a vision screening the same as an eye exam?
    No. Screenings at school, at the doctor’s office or for a driver’s license can find some problems, but the AOA says they can’t replace a comprehensive eye exam, the only effective way to confirm or rule out an eye disease or vision problem.
    How often should I get an eye exam if I wear contacts?
    Every year. The AAO says contact lens wearers should see an eye specialist yearly, and the AOA counts them among the patients who need an exam at least once a year.
    How often should someone with diabetes get an eye exam?
    At least once a year. The AAO recommends the first exam at diagnosis for type 2 diabetes and five years after onset for type 1, then yearly; pregnancy changes the schedule, so ask your eye doctor.
    Does an eye exam include a glasses prescription?
    If it includes a refraction, the test that measures your prescription, yes: under the FTC’s Eyeglass Rule your eye doctor must give you a copy right away, at no extra charge, whether you ask or not.
