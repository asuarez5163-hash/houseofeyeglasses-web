    How long is an eyeglass prescription valid?

    Usually one or two years from your eye exam. In the United States, state law decides when a glasses prescription expires, and your eye doctor writes the date on it; the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) says glasses prescriptions are usually good for a year or two, but state laws vary. The date on your paper is the one that counts.

    Where to find the date

    Many prescriptions show the exam date and an expiration date, often near the doctor’s signature. If yours shows only the exam date, ask the office that wrote it how long it is valid in your state. How to read your prescription explains every other number on the paper.

    Why prescriptions expire

    Eyes change. Nearsightedness usually starts between ages 6 and 14 and gets worse until the early twenties, and presbyopia gets worse over time until it usually stops after 65, the National Eye Institute says; astigmatism can change gradually too, according to the American Academy of Ophthalmology. An exam also checks the health of your eyes, not just the numbers: many eye and vision problems have no obvious signs, the American Optometric Association notes.

    Your right to a copy

    Under the FTC’s Eyeglass Rule, your eye doctor must give you a copy of your glasses prescription immediately after an eye exam that includes a refraction, whether you ask for it or not, at no extra charge. They can’t require you to buy glasses from them, and they can’t ask you to sign a waiver or liability release to get it. They may offer a digital copy, but you have the right to a paper one, and if they sell glasses they must ask you to confirm that you received it.

    If you think a prescriber is breaking the rule, the FTC asks you to report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

    The PD may not be on it

    Your pupillary distance (PD) tells the lab where to center each lens. The FTC notes that most states do not require it on the prescription, and that in some states the prescriber can charge for measuring it. If yours doesn’t have it, measure your PD at home with a ruler, or ask your eye doctor.

    Contact lens prescriptions are different

    A contact lens prescription is valid for at least one year unless your prescriber has a medical reason to make it shorter (FTC). It is a separate prescription, written after a contact lens fitting, and it can’t be used to make glasses. More in contact lenses vs glasses.

    Ordering with us

    We make lenses only from a current prescription that has not expired. If yours has expired, see an eye doctor for a new exam first; we don’t do eye exams. Then type your numbers or upload a photo of the prescription in the lens step of any frame.

    Questions

    Good to know

    Can I order glasses with an expired prescription?
    Not here. We make lenses only from a current prescription. If yours has expired, get a new eye exam: your vision may have changed since the last one.
    Does my eye doctor have to give me my prescription?
    Yes. Under the FTC’s Eyeglass Rule, after an eye exam that includes a refraction, your prescriber must give you a copy at no extra charge, even if you don’t ask. They can ask you to pay for the exam first only if they ask that of every patient.
    What if my prescription has no expiration date?
    Ask the office that wrote it how long it is valid in your state. The FTC notes that glasses prescriptions are usually good for a year or two, but state laws vary.
    Is a contact lens prescription the same as a glasses prescription?
    No. A contact lens prescription names the lens manufacturer or brand, its base curve and, when needed, its diameter, and it is valid for at least a year unless there is a medical reason for less. Glasses need their own prescription.
