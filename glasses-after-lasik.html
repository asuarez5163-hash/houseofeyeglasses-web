    Do you still need glasses after LASIK?

    For everyday distance vision, often not. But many people still need glasses for something: LASIK cannot correct presbyopia, so almost everyone with good distance vision needs reading glasses after about 40, and some people are left with a small prescription or with glare at night. Your surgeon is the one to ask about your own eyes.

    Reading glasses after 40

    LASIK reshapes the cornea, the clear front of the eye. Presbyopia happens inside the eye: with age, the natural lens gets harder and less flexible and stops focusing up close. The American Academy of Ophthalmology (AAO) says LASIK cannot correct presbyopia, and that with or without refractive surgery, almost everyone who has excellent distance vision will need reading glasses after around age 40.

    If you only need help up close, your eye doctor can write a reading prescription, and single vision lenses made with those numbers become reading glasses fitted to each of your eyes. If you need help far and near, progressive lenses cover distance, the computer and reading in one pair, with no line.

    A small prescription left over

    LASIK can leave an eye slightly under- or over-corrected. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) lists this among the risks of the surgery and says you may still need glasses or contact lenses afterwards; the AAO adds that these problems often can be improved with glasses, contact lenses or additional laser surgery.

    A prescription written after LASIK is a regular glasses prescription, and we make it like any other, as single vision or progressive lenses, in any frame we carry. A prescription from before the surgery no longer matches your eyes, so don’t use it.

    Glare and night driving

    Some people see glare, halos or starbursts around lights after LASIK. The AAO says side effects like these usually go away over time but, in rare cases, may not; the FDA says some patients develop glare, halos or double vision that can seriously affect nighttime vision. The AAO also notes that even after LASIK you might need glasses for certain activities, such as reading or driving at night.

    If your eye doctor prescribes glasses for driving at night, an anti-reflective coating keeps the lenses themselves from adding reflections. It does not treat glare that comes from the eye: that is a question for your surgeon.

    Dry eyes and sunglasses

    Dry eyes are common after LASIK. The AAO says almost everyone who has it will have dry eyes and changing vision during the day, usually fading within a month, though for some people it takes longer; the FDA notes that some patients develop severe dry eye that may be permanent. Follow your surgeon’s instructions on drops and eye protection while you heal.

    If you still wear a prescription, we can make it as prescription sunglasses with a solid or gradient sun tint, or as light-responsive lenses that darken outdoors. We don’t make sun lenses without a prescription.

    Keep up your eye exams

    LASIK changes how your eye focuses; it doesn’t replace the checkups that look at the health of your eyes. The American Optometric Association (AOA) says people who have had refractive surgery (LASIK, PRK or SMILE) should still have an eye exam every 1–2 years. More in how often to get an eye exam.

    Questions

    Good to know

    Will I need reading glasses after LASIK?
    Most likely, at some point. LASIK cannot correct presbyopia, the age-related loss of close-up focus that shows up in your 40s. Some people choose monovision LASIK, with one eye set for distance and the other slightly nearsighted for close work; the AAO notes it is not for everyone.
    Can I use my old glasses prescription after LASIK?
    No. LASIK changes the way your eyes focus, so a prescription written before surgery no longer fits them. If you need glasses afterwards, get a new eye exam and order with the new prescription.
    How soon after LASIK can I get glasses?
    Ask your surgeon when your vision is stable enough for a glasses prescription. Your eyes keep settling for a while after surgery: before a second laser treatment, for example, the FDA advises waiting until your measurements are the same at two visits at least three months apart.
    Do I need glasses for night driving after LASIK?
    Only if your eye doctor says so. Some people see glare or halos at night after LASIK, and the AAO notes you might need glasses for activities such as driving at night. If your doctor writes a prescription for it, we make the lenses, with an anti-reflective coating if you want one.
