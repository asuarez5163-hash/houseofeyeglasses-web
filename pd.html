    Measure your PD online

    Your PD (pupillary distance) is the distance between the centers of your pupils, in millimeters. The lab uses it to center each lens in front of your eye. Most adults measure between 50 and 75 mm, about 63 mm on average; kids measure less. Measure yours below with your camera in about a minute, or with a ruler and a mirror.

    How the camera measures

    A face detector running in your browser finds the centers of your pupils, the edges of your irises (the colored part of the eye) and the shape of your face. It turns the distance between your pupils into millimeters with two rulers: the iris, about 11.7 mm across in most adults, and the proportions of your face compared with an average adult face. Each ruler is off for some people, so we weigh the two by how much each one varies from person to person. For a child we use the iris alone, because a child’s face is smaller than the average adult face. Each take averages about a second of video; we take three and keep the middle one, and if they disagree by more than 1.5 mm we ask you to measure again.

    Looking at a camera up close makes your eyes turn in a little, so your pupils sit closer together than when you look far away. We estimate how far you are and add that difference back, usually 1 to 2 mm, so the result is your distance PD, the one your glasses are made with. The two numbers per eye are measured from the middle of your nose, halfway between the inner corners of your eyes.

    How accurate is it?

    Count on about ±2.5 mm for an adult and ±3 mm for a child. Iris width and face size both vary from person to person: in Google’s test of the iris as a ruler, with more than 200 people, it was off by 4.3% on average, and face width varies about as much between adults. Weighing the two rulers brings the typical error to about 3.5%, roughly 2.5 mm on a 63 mm PD, and about one measurement in twenty can still be off by 4 to 5 mm. The split between your two eyes is less certain than the total.

    In published comparisons with an optician’s pupillometer, the two best phone apps of three were off by about half a millimeter on average, while another study found an app that read 0.6 mm small on average, with single measurements up to about 3 mm off. Those were iPhone apps; a web page like this one works only from the ordinary camera image, so don’t expect it to do better.

    For progressive lenses or a strong prescription, use the PD from your eye doctor or optician: there a millimeter matters more. A licensed optician checks every prescription before the lab starts.

    How to measure your PD with a ruler

    No camera, or rather not use it? You need a ruler with millimeters and a few minutes. For one number per eye (dual PD), line up 0 with the middle of your nose and read the mark at the center of each pupil.

    With a mirror

    1. Stand about 8 inches (20 cm) from a mirror, in good light. Hold a millimeter ruler flat against your brow, just above your eyes.
    2. Close your right eye. Line up the ruler’s 0 with the center of your left pupil.
    3. Keep the ruler still. Close your left eye, open your right, and read the mark at the center of your right pupil. That number is your PD.
    4. Repeat three times and use the average.

    With a friend

    1. Sit face to face at the same height, about an arm’s length apart.
    2. Look past your friend at something at least 10 feet (3 m) away, and keep your eyes there.
    3. Your friend holds the ruler against your brow, lines up 0 with the center of one pupil and reads the mark at the center of the other.
    4. Measure three times and use the average.

    Your camera stays on your device

    The video never leaves your phone or computer: the face detector runs inside your browser, and no picture is uploaded or saved. The first time, your browser downloads the detector (about 10 MB); nothing goes the other way. The camera turns off when the three takes are done.

    The only thing kept is the result, and only if you press “Use this PD”: three numbers saved in this browser so the lens form can fill them in for you during the next 7 days.

    Questions

    Good to know

    Single PD or dual PD: which do I need?
    A single PD is one number, from pupil to pupil (63). A dual PD is two numbers, one per eye, from the middle of your nose to each pupil (31.5/31.5); it centers each lens on its own eye, which matters most for progressives and strong prescriptions. This tool gives you both, and the lens form takes either.
    What is near PD?
    The PD for reading: it is smaller than your distance PD because your eyes turn in to read up close. Two numbers like 63/60 on a prescription usually mean distance and near PD, not right and left. This tool measures your distance PD.
    Is an online PD measurement accurate?
    Count on about ±2.5 mm for an adult if you follow the steps: glasses off, good light, about an arm’s length away and your eyes on the camera. The tool measures three times and asks again if the takes disagree. An optician’s pupillometer is more precise.
    Can I use it for progressive lenses?
    We recommend the PD from your eye doctor or optician for progressives and for strong prescriptions, where a millimeter matters more. For single-vision lenses, a careful measurement here or with a ruler is a good place to start, and a licensed optician checks every prescription before the lab starts.
